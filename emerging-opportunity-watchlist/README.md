# Lightweight Emerging-Opportunity Watchlist — IIH / SwiftServe

**Purpose:** Give one person a repeatable way to spot useful opportunities for IIH and SwiftServe without needing a dedicated researcher.

**Run window:** 20–26 October 2026  
**Review date:** 26 October 2026  
**Weekly time budget:** about 90 minutes

## 1. The weekly system

### 90-minute workflow
- **0–10 min — Set-up:** open the watchlist and check deadlines or follow-ups from the previous week.
- **10–60 min — Scan 10 sources:** about 5 minutes per source; only capture items that could affect funding, partnerships, pilots, users, policy, research, or market access.
- **60–75 min — Score and flag:** apply the criteria below.
- **75–85 min — Validate:** open the original source for every flagged item and confirm eligibility, deadline, geography, and required action.
- **85–90 min — Decide and schedule:** move each flagged item to **Pursue**, **Watch**, or **Drop** and record the next action.

### What gets recorded
Each item is logged with: **date checked, source, opportunity, type, deadline/status, relevance to IIH/SwiftServe, score, flag, and next action.**

### Flag criteria
Score each item out of **7**:
- **Relevance (0–2):** directly helps SwiftServe/IIH or a target capability.
- **Actionability (0–2):** a real application, partnership, event, procurement, research, or pilot action is available.
- **Fit (0–2):** Nigeria/Africa, eligible organisation/person, and realistic stage/sector fit.
- **Credibility (0–1):** primary/official source and enough detail to verify.

**Flag for closer look at 5/7 or higher.**  
A high score does not mean “apply”; it means “spend another 10–15 minutes validating and deciding.”

## 4. Decision rule after a flag

**Capture → Score → Validate → Decide**

For every item scoring **5/7+**:
1. Open the primary source.
2. Confirm eligibility, deadline and required materials.
3. Ask: **Does this move IIH/SwiftServe forward within the next 30–60 days?**
4. Choose one outcome:
   - **Pursue:** assign a next action and deadline.
   - **Watch:** save the source and define the next check date.
   - **Drop:** record the reason so the same noise is not re-reviewed.

## 5. Reflection from the test run

**What worked:** Official government and development-partner pages produced the most actionable signals. The scoring rule quickly separated direct opportunities (SMEDAN) from ecosystem signals (World Bank) and sector-specific programmes (NIMASA).

**What was noise:** Closed calls and opportunities with the wrong commercial role/sector took time without producing a usable next action. The NITDA AI challenge and the AfDB consultancy EOI were useful signals but not immediate SwiftServe opportunities.

**What I would change next week:** Keep the 10-source list, but give SMEDAN/NITDA/AfDB/NIMASA more weight because they produced the strongest Nigeria-focused signals. Add a simple **deadline-under-7-days** tag so urgent opportunities rise to the top automatically.

**Test conclusion:** The watchlist is small enough for one person to run in about 90 minutes a week and produced **3 flagged items out of 6 logged items** in this test window.
